The sign-off service of SPEC Section 8.3 synthesises the submitted RTL with GPDK045 under the Section 6 constraints, and simulates the netlists with SDF back-annotation against its own testbench, which samples outputs at the rising edge. Two submissions were made. `check/400` (commit 60ed005) carried the 400 MHz design while `rtl_800/` was still empty. `check/800` (commit b6917e9) carried both final designs, whose RTL has not changed since. The service was online throughout the session, but the session's environment had not been set up to trigger it. Once that was corrected, both scorecards were published on the branch `check-results` [16], checked at 06:37 and 06:48 UTC.

The functional results agree completely. Both designs pass an independent testbench on a different library and with a different synthesis tool, and that testbench samples at the rising edge where ours samples at the falling edge. The informational failure case matches as well. Both flows ran the 400 MHz netlist at 1.25 ns and saw no product return, but the sign-off simulator reports the violations directly as timing-check messages, while Icarus shows them only through their effect on the data.

Timing agrees in kind but not in detail. At 400 MHz the two slacks are almost equal, while at 800 MHz GPDK045 has twice the margin. The scorecard does not state its corner. A commercial synthesis tool typically closes timing to the constraint and then recovers area and power with the slack it has left, so its reported slack tends to sit just above the target and says little about raw gate speed. The 800 MHz design was cut for 0.75 ns of logic per stage at a slow 0.95 V, 125 °C corner, so it has more stage margin to give at whatever corner GPDK045 uses.

Area is 15 % (400 MHz) and 19 % (800 MHz) larger in GPDK045, with the larger excess on the flip-flop-heavy design. This is consistent with larger sequential cells in that library, though the scorecard gives no cell breakdown. The vectorless power is 2.4 to 2.9 times lower in GPDK045. The two estimates use different libraries, supply voltages and default activities, so the ratio cannot be attributed to the designs. The ranking agrees, however, with the 800 MHz design drawing four to five times the power of the 400 MHz design in both flows. The open-source figures are therefore the pessimistic ones, and the design decisions in this report hold under both flows.
